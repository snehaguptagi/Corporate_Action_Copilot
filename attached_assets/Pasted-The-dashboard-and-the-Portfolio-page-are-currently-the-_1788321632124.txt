The dashboard and the Portfolio page are currently the same page twice. Fix the
bugs, then give the dashboard its own job and its own visual language.

═══ PART 1 — BUGS ═══

1. THE NAV RENDERS TWICE. A DOM scan finds Dashboard, Portfolio, Corporate
   actions and Analysis each appearing twice, each with its own icon — the
   desktop sidebar and the mobile sheet are both mounted at all times. That is
   the duplicate icon. Render one: mount the mobile nav only below its
   breakpoint, or share a single list rendered once.

2. THE HEADLINE SENTENCE IS STILL LOGICALLY BROKEN.
   It reads: "4 notices in the last 24 hours. 31 affect your schemes."
   There are 31 open corporate actions in total, so "31 affect" is an all-time
   count inside a sentence about the last 24 hours. It parses as "31 of the 4".
   Either both numbers describe the same window, or they are separate
   statements. This is the third time this sentence has shipped broken — add a
   test asserting the second number can never exceed the first when both
   describe the same window.

3. THE NEAREST DEADLINE IS THREE MONTHS IN THE PAST.
   /api/dashboard returns nearestDeadline "15 Jun 2026 · 15:00 IST" and
   nearestFundingIssuer "Coastal Energy Ltd". Today is 2 Sep 2026. A closed or
   expired event is being selected as "nearest". Filter to FUTURE deadlines on
   OPEN events only, and add a test that the nearest deadline is always after
   now.

4. THE SAME THREE NUMBERS APPEAR THREE TIMES. The sentence says 4 notices, then
   two tiles say "Last 24 hours 4 / Affect Arka 31", then a "CONTROL PULSE"
   block says "NOTICES 4 / SCHEMES TOUCHED 8/10 / NEEDS ATTENTION 9". Keep ONE
   representation. The sentence is the best of the three; delete the duplicate
   tile strips.

5. REMOVE "YOUR SCHEMES" FROM THE DASHBOARD. It is the Portfolio page,
   duplicated. The dashboard links to Portfolio; it does not contain it.

6. Materiality is blank on 3 of 5 inbound rows because those events are
   value-neutral. Blank reads as missing data — show the cash figure or
   "Neutral", consistently.

═══ PART 2 — WHAT THE DASHBOARD IS FOR ═══
Rename the H1. "Portfolio operations" says nothing and collides with the
Portfolio page. Make the H1 state the situation, the way the corporate action
page already does well:

    "9 corporate actions need you. ₹24.24 cr to fund by 16 Sep."

That is a heading that both names the page's job and is useful. Keep the
eyebrow as the product name.

The division of labour:
    DASHBOARD  the whole book at a glance, ORIENTED AROUND TIME AND MONEY.
               Graphic, wide, few words. Answers "what is coming at me".
    PORTFOLIO  a dense per-scheme ledger. Tabular, no charts.
               Answers "is each of my funds okay".
They must not look the same. Dashboard is a control screen; Portfolio is a
ledger.

═══ PART 3 — WHAT TO SHOW (charts are now wanted, but earn each one) ═══
Nothing in this product currently shows TIME, and corporate actions are
entirely deadline-driven. That is the gap. Build these four, in this order:

A. DEADLINE TIMELINE — next 30 days
   The single most useful thing missing. A horizontal time axis, today at the
   left, one marker per open event at its decision deadline. Marker carries the
   issuer and whether a decision is required. Clustering is the insight — three
   deadlines landing the same week is invisible in a table and obvious here.
   One series, so no legend; the title names it.

B. FUNDING BY WEEK
   Bar chart, one bar per week for the next six weeks, height = rupees due that
   week. This is liquidity planning, and it is the question a fund manager
   actually asks. Single series in the accent hue. Label the bars directly; no
   y-axis gridlines beyond a faint baseline.

C. HEADROOM AGAINST THE SEBI CAP — per scheme
   Horizontal bars, one per scheme, showing how much of the 10% single-issuer
   allowance the largest issuer consumes. Sorted descending, so Focused 25 sits
   at the top near the limit. Draw the 10% limit as a thin rule across the
   chart. Use the status colours ONLY where a scheme is tight or breaching —
   everything else in the accent or a neutral.

D. VOLUME VERSUS ATTENTION — the insight, not a pie
   Two stacked horizontal bars, one above the other:
     by COUNT           dividends dominate
     by VALUE AT RISK   rights issues dominate
   Same five event-type categories, same fixed colour order in both bars, so
   the reader sees the inversion: the majority of arrivals need no decision,
   and a small minority carries all the money. State that in one line beneath.
   Do NOT use a donut or a pie for this.

Then, unchanged in purpose: the attention queue (what needs a decision, ranked)
and the inbound corporate actions table.

CHART RULES — these are not stylistic preferences:
   - No dual-axis charts, ever. Two measures of different scale become two
     charts, as in D above.
   - Categorical colours assigned in a FIXED order and never cycled; if event
     types exceed the palette, fold the tail into "Other".
   - Sequential magnitude = one hue, light to dark. Never a rainbow.
   - Status colours (good / tight / breach) are reserved for state and must
     never be reused as a series colour.
   - Every chart with two or more series carries a legend; identity is never
     conveyed by colour alone. Text stays in ink tokens, never the series
     colour.
   - Thin marks, recessive grid and axes, 2px gaps between adjacent fills,
     direct labels on selected points only — never a number on every point.
   - Each chart gets a hover tooltip.
   - Provide a table fallback for each chart, and verify the palette works in
     the warm light theme rather than assuming it does.
   - If a number is better as a single figure than a plot, make it a stat tile.
     The captured/forfeited/at-stake scoreboard is tiles, not a chart.

- Do not touch the corporate action page's sections or any Stage 1 figure.
- Do not re-add a scheme table to the dashboard.
- Do not use a pie or donut anywhere.
