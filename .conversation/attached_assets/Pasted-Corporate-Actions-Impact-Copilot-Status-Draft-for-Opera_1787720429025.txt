Corporate Actions Impact Copilot
Status: Draft for Operations, Product, Risk and Technology review
Primary user: Corporate Actions Analyst
Product type: Internal operations workbench
Purpose: Reduce the manual effort and operational risk involved in processing corporate actions.

1. Product summary
The Corporate Actions Impact Copilot will help Operations process corporate actions from the moment a notice is received until the expected cash or securities have been reconciled.
The product is not simply a notice summariser. It connects the complete operating process:
Notice → Event → Affected accounts → Impact → Election, if required → Instruction → Settlement → Reconciliation → Audit
The Copilot will support multiple event types, including dividends, stock splits, rights issues, tender offers, mergers, redemptions and optional events.
A common platform will manage notice intake, holdings matching, deadlines, tasks, approvals and audit history. Event-specific modules will define which terms, calculations and elections are required for each corporate-action type.
AI will help read and organise information. Deterministic code will perform calculations and workflow checks. Authorised users will retain responsibility for validation, elections and approvals.

2. Problem statement
Corporate-action notices arrive through different sources and formats. They may contain long narratives, amendments, multiple dates, market-specific terminology and different options.
Receiving the notice is only the beginning. The analyst must still:
Identify the event and affected security.
Understand the relevant dates, rates, ratios, prices and options.
Find every affected fund and account.
Calculate the expected cash, securities or funding requirement.
Obtain an election where the event requires a choice.
Monitor internal and external deadlines.
Prepare and track the instruction.
Reconcile the final settlement.
Retain evidence of every material action.
This work is often distributed across notices, spreadsheets, emails and operational systems. As a result, teams face several risks:
An affected fund may be missed.
An outdated notice may be used.
A critical term may be copied incorrectly.
A calculation may use the wrong rule.
An election may not be received before the deadline.
An instruction may be rejected or remain unconfirmed.
The final settlement may not match the expected result.
The reasoning behind a decision may be difficult to reconstruct.
The Copilot should create one controlled record connecting the notice, event terms, affected accounts, calculations, elections, instructions and settlement.

3. Product goals
The product should:
Convert notices into structured corporate-action events.
Link every critical event term to source evidence.
Identify every affected fund and account.
Apply the correct calculation for the identified event.
Separate mandatory events from events requiring an election.
Track elections, deadlines, approvals and instructions.
Highlight missing, contradictory or urgent information.
Generate clear downstream tasks.
Compare expected and actual settlement.
Retain a complete audit history.
The product should reduce manual preparation work while keeping important decisions with authorised people.

4. Users and responsibilities
Corporate Actions Analyst — primary user
The analyst reviews notices, validates event terms, investigates unclear information, confirms affected accounts and manages the event through completion.
Checker or Team Lead
The checker approves high-risk cases, material corrections, overrides and instruction-ready events where maker-checker control is required.
Fund Manager or Authorised Delegate
This person makes the fund-level election for voluntary events or mandatory events with options.
Relationship Manager
The RM may coordinate communications where the operating model requires client, fund or portfolio-manager involvement. The RM is not the primary operations user.
Reconciliation or Fund Accounting Team
This team investigates differences between the expected and actual cash or securities.
Product and Technology Team
This team maintains the extraction logic, calculations, workflow rules, integrations and audit controls.

6. End-to-end user flow
Stage 1: Receive and register the notice
The system receives a PDF, text file or structured message.
It stores:
Original document.
Source and channel.
Received date and time.
File checksum.
External event reference.
Document version.
Document role: new, preliminary, amended, cancelled, status or confirmation.
The original document must never be overwritten.
Stage 2: Classify the event
The system identifies:
Event type.
Processing type.
Security.
Whether the document relates to an existing event.
Whether analyst review is required.
The result must include the evidence used for classification.
Stage 3: Extract event-specific terms
The system selects the relevant field template for the event.
Common fields include:
Issuer.
ISIN or other security identifier.
Event reference.
Announcement date.
Ex date.
Record date.
Payment or effective date.
Market deadline and time zone.
Currency.
Options.
Default result.
Instruction destination.
Additional fields depend on the event. For example:
Dividend: cash rate and tax information.
Split: pre-split and post-split ratio.
Rights issue: rights ratio and subscription price.
Tender: offer price and tender conditions.
Merger: exchange ratio and cash consideration.
Every critical term must include its source document, page or message location, supporting text, confidence and review status.
Stage 4: Validate the terms
Deterministic checks confirm that:
Required fields are present.
Dates are logically consistent.
The security identifier is valid.
Currency is known.
Rates and ratios can be parsed.
The market deadline includes the correct time zone.
Event options and default consequences are clear.
The notice does not conflict with a later version.
If a critical term is missing or contradictory, the event moves to Needs Review. The system must not guess the value.
Stage 5: Identify affected accounts
The validated security is matched against the security master and approved holdings snapshot.
The system verifies:
One valid security match exists.
The holdings snapshot is for the correct eligibility date.
All positions for the security are included.
Eligible quantity is present.
Required fund and account attributes are available.
The result is one impact record for each affected fund or account.
Stage 6: Calculate the impact
The system selects the event-specific calculation.
Examples:
Cash dividend: eligible quantity × dividend rate
Stock split: eligible quantity × split ratio
Rights issue: eligible quantity × rights ratio
Rights subscription cash: exercisable rights × subscription price
Merger: eligible quantity × exchange ratio
Redemption: eligible quantity × redemption rate
Calculations must be performed by deterministic code.
Each result must store:
Input values.
Formula.
Fraction or rounding rule.
Rule version.
Output.
Calculation time.
Stage 7: Manage elections
If the event is mandatory and has no options, the process moves directly to expected settlement and task creation.
If an election is required, the system creates one checklist row per affected account.
The checklist includes:
Fund and account.
Eligible position.
Valid options.
Calculated result for each option.
Default option.
Market deadline.
Internal deadline.
Election owner.
Election status.
Approval status.
Instruction status.
Risk flags.
The Copilot may explain the effect of each option. It must not select the final election.
Stage 8: Prioritise and create tasks
The system creates tasks when:
A critical term needs validation.
A security or account cannot be matched.
An election is missing.
A deadline is approaching.
A default outcome may cause a loss.
A checker’s approval is required.
An instruction has been rejected.
Confirmation has not been received.
Settlement does not match expectations.
Priority must be based on transparent rules such as time remaining, event value, missing information, default consequence and approval state.
Stage 9: Prepare and track instructions
Once the event is complete and approved, the system prepares an instruction in the required format.
For the POC, instructions will be clearly marked:
DRAFT — NOT SUBMITTED
The POC may simulate accepted, rejected and pending statuses. It will not submit a live market instruction.
Stage 10: Reconcile and close
The system calculates the expected cash or securities and compares them with the settlement or booking record.
A difference outside the approved tolerance creates an exception.
The event can be closed only when:
Expected and actual results agree; or
The difference has been investigated and resolved; or
An authorised user records an approved closure reason.

8. Product interface
Event Inbox
Shows all events with security, event type, processing type, market deadline, internal deadline, status and risk.
Notice Review
Shows the original notice beside extracted fields, supporting evidence, confidence and analyst corrections.
Impact Workbench
Shows affected funds, holdings, calculation inputs, formulas and expected results.
Election Workbench
Appears for events requiring a decision. It shows options, defaults, owners, elections, approvals and deadlines.
Tasks and Risks
Shows unresolved information, missing elections, urgent deadlines, rejected instructions, settlement exceptions and required next actions.
Audit and Reconciliation
Shows notice versions, changes, calculations, elections, approvals, instructions and expected-versus-actual settlement.

9. AI, rules and human controls
AI may:
Classify notices.
Extract terms.
Find supporting evidence.
Compare notice versions.
Explain an event.
Draft notes and task descriptions.
Deterministic code must:
Match securities and holdings.
Perform financial calculations.
Apply deadline and priority rules.
Enforce workflow states.
Validate required fields.
Record audit events.
Humans must:
Validate critical terms.
Resolve ambiguity and conflicts.
Make fund elections.
Approve high-risk cases.
Authorise instructions.
Approve material overrides.
The operating principle is:
AI prepares the case, rules control the process, and humans remain accountable.

10. POC scope
The POC should prove that one common platform can handle different corporate-action workflows.
It should include:
Cash dividend.
Stock split.
Rights issue.
Optional dividend or tender offer.
Amended notice.
Duplicate notice.
Ambiguous critical term.
Settlement match.
Settlement break.
The POC will use sample, synthetic or de-identified notices and holdings.
It will not attempt to support every market variation.

11. POC data requirements
The business must provide:
Eight to twelve sample notices.
Holdings for approximately 10–20 funds.
Both affected and unaffected accounts.
Security identifiers and record dates.
Event-specific calculation rules.
Fraction and rounding rules.
Internal deadline rules.
Election and approval rules.
Expected extraction results.
Expected affected accounts.
Expected calculations.
Synthetic settlement records.
At least two experienced Operations analysts should approve the expected answers before development begins.

12. Success criteria
The POC succeeds if it demonstrates:
Correct classification of supported event types.
Every critical term linked to supporting evidence.
All affected funds identified.
No unaffected funds incorrectly included.
Exact calculation results.
Correct distinction between mandatory and elective events.
Missing elections and urgent deadlines flagged.
Amendments reopening dependent calculations and approvals.
Correct task and escalation creation.
Settlement differences correctly identified.
Complete audit reconstruction.
No AI-selected election.
No live instruction submission.
A measurable reduction in analyst preparation time.

13. Out of scope for the POC
Live SWIFT, custodian or CSD integration.
Live instruction submission.
Production client data.
Every corporate-action event and market.
Complex tax and withholding-relief processing.
Legal interpretation.
Autonomous elections.
Customer-facing functionality.
Full production security, resilience and scale certification.
NAV Exception Copilot and Operations Knowledge Copilot.

14. Decisions required before development
The build should not start until the business confirms:
Supported POC event types.
Sample notice and holdings pack.
Critical fields for each event.
Security and account matching rules.
Calculation, fraction and rounding rules.
Internal deadline logic.
Election authority.
Maker-checker requirements.
Priority and escalation rules.
Reconciliation tolerance.
Acceptance criteria.
Named Operations reviewers.
Final product definition
The Corporate Actions Impact Copilot is a controlled internal operations workbench that reads different corporate-action notices, identifies affected funds, calculates event-specific impacts, manages elections and deadlines, prepares the next operational actions, tracks settlement and retains the complete audit history.
