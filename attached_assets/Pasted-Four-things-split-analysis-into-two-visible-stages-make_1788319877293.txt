Four things: split analysis into two visible stages, make the corporate action's
resting state be real cached data, make cross-event analysis explain itself, and
resolve what Audit is for.

═══ 1. TWO STAGES OF ANALYSIS, RUN IN ORDER, VISIBLY SEPARATE ═══
Every corporate action must be analysed twice, in this sequence, and the
frontend must show them as two distinct labelled stages — not blended:

  STAGE 1 · DETERMINISTIC   the maths
    entitlement, rounding, eligible schemes, cash required or receivable,
    NAV paise per unit, TERP, value of a right, SEBI issuer exposure before
    and after, cash headroom, permitted maximum.
    No model call. Reproducible. Same inputs always give the same output.

  STAGE 2 · AI JUDGEMENT    the interpretation
    reads Stage 1's OUTPUT plus the portfolio context and writes prose:
    the trade-off, the recommendation, the risk, what to do first.

Label them exactly that way in the UI so the fund manager can see which numbers
are computed and which words are inferred. Stage 2 must visibly come after
Stage 1 and must state which model produced it and when.

═══ 2. THE GUARDRAIL — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PART ═══
Stage 2 must be structurally incapable of changing a number.

  - Stage 2 receives Stage 1's computed figures as its ONLY numeric input.
  - Stage 2 may cite those figures. It may NOT introduce a figure of its own,
    recompute one, or contradict one.
  - Validate the model output before rendering: extract every number from the
    response and assert each one appears in Stage 1's output. If a number is
    not there, reject the response and show Stage 1 alone with a note that
    judgement is unavailable.
  - Never let Stage 2 output feed a calculation, an election, an instruction or
    an approval.

Add a test that feeds a deliberately hallucinated figure through the validator
and asserts it is rejected.

═══ 3. WHAT STAGE 2 SHOULD ACTUALLY CONTRIBUTE ═══
Otherwise it is decoration. It must say things the maths cannot:

  - the trade-off, in one sentence: "Exercising in full breaches the cap.
    Selling the excess 7,72,993 rights on exchange recovers about ₹2.25 cr of
    the ₹2.51 cr at stake, so sell rather than lapse."
  - cross-event judgement: "Three of your schemes hold this issuer. The bonus
    issue raises that exposure and you cannot decline it."
  - what to do first, with a reason that is not just size: "Act on Bharat
    first — not because it is largest, but because the rights-entitlement
    window closes five days before the subscription deadline."
  - precedent from history: "You lapsed two rights issues last quarter worth
    ₹18 lakh, both in schemes that had cash available."
  - what is MISSING from the notice: "The notice does not state the RE trading
    window. Confirm with SBI-SG before relying on the sell option."

That last one is the most valuable — the model noticing an absence in the data.

Keep it short. Four or five sentences per corporate action, not an essay.

═══ 4. LIVE DATA IS THE RESTING STATE, NOT DEMO DATA ═══
What a corporate action shows before any fetch must be the LAST FETCHED LIVE
DATA, cached — not synthetic seed data.

Build it as: fetch -> store raw -> extract -> Stage 1 -> Stage 2, each step
persisted, so the page renders the last completed run instantly with no waiting.

  - Store the raw provider response verbatim, immutably, with a fetch timestamp.
  - Extract the fields the product needs from that raw payload; keep the raw
    copy so extraction can be re-run without re-fetching.
  - Show "as of <timestamp>" on every corporate action, and how it arrived.
  - A manual "Refresh" re-runs the chain and shows what changed.
  - Where nothing has been fetched yet, SAY SO plainly and label the record
    synthetic. Never present seeded data as though it were fetched. Do not mix
    a real record and a synthetic one in the same list without marking which is
    which.

I still need the market-data provider name and docs before the fetch layer can
be written — build the cache, extraction and staging structure now with a
clearly isolated provider adapter so the real one drops in without touching
anything else.

═══ 5. CROSS-EVENT ANALYSIS DOES NOT EXPLAIN ITSELF ═══
It currently opens with a table of percentages per issuer per scheme. Nothing
tells the reader why the page exists or which row matters, so it reads as data
rather than a finding.

Lead with the conclusion in words, then the table as evidence — the same
pattern that already works on the corporate action page and the scheme page:

  "Arka Focused 25 will breach the SEBI single-issuer cap on Bharat Renewables
   if you exercise in full — 10.77% against a 10% limit. Separately, Saffron
   Digital leaves the same scheme 0.21% from the cap on a MANDATORY event it
   cannot decline. No other scheme is within 2% of a limit."

And say in one line why anyone should care, because right now it is unstated:
each corporate action is checked on its own, but a scheme holding one issuer
across several concurrent events can breach on the combination. That is the
only thing this page exists to catch. Say it.

If nothing is at risk, say that too: "No scheme is within 2% of a
single-issuer limit."

═══ 6. RESOLVE AUDIT ═══
A raw action log is not useful to a fund manager as a destination, and its
purpose is currently unexplained.

  - Remove "Audit" from the navigation.
  - Keep the data. Per-event history already exists as section 06 on the
    corporate action page; that is where it belongs.
  - Move the part a fund manager wants into the Analysis history section and
    frame it as decisions rather than events: what we decided, when, who
    approved it, and what it was worth. That is the quarter-end question.
  - Retain a compliance export — the full trail, downloadable — because SEBI
    stewardship disclosure needs it. Reachable from Analysis, not the main nav.

═══ DO NOT ═══
- Do not let Stage 2 write to any figure, election, instruction or approval.
- Do not blend the two stages into one block of text.
- Do not add charts.
- Do not present synthetic data without labelling it synthetic.
- Do not change any Stage 1 figure — the deterministic maths is correct and
  tested.

═══ VERIFY ═══
typecheck and tests pass, plus:
- every corporate action page shows Stage 1 and Stage 2 as separate labelled
  sections, in that order, with the model and timestamp named on Stage 2;
- the validator rejects a Stage 2 response containing a number absent from
  Stage 1, and the page still renders Stage 1 alone;
- Stage 1 output is identical across repeated runs with no model call;
- each corporate action shows "as of <timestamp>" and its arrival channel;
- any record with no completed fetch is labelled synthetic;
- cross-event analysis opens with a written conclusion naming the breaching
  scheme and issuer, and states why combination risk matters;
- Audit is gone from the nav, per-event history still renders, and a compliance
  export is reachable from Analysis.
