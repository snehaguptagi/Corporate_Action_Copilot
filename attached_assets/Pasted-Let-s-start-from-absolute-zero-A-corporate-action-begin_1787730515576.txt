Let’s start from absolute zero.

A corporate action begins when a company decides to do something that affects the people who hold its shares or bonds.

Examples:

- Pay a dividend
- Split its shares
- Offer additional shares through a rights issue
- Buy back shares through a tender offer
- Merge with another company

The complete real-life flow is roughly:

```text
Company
   ↓
Issuer’s agent / registrar / paying agent
   ↓
Stock exchange, depository or market infrastructure
   ↓
Custodian
   ↓
Fund operations team
   ↓
Funds and accounts holding the security
   ↓
Instruction, payment or security movement
```

## 1. What does a corporate-action notice look like?

A corporate-action notice is normally a structured message or document announcing the event and its terms.

Fund operations may receive the information through:

- A custodian portal
- A SWIFT message
- An ISO 20022 message
- A market-data vendor
- An email or attached PDF
- A stock-exchange or depository feed

ISO 20022 formally defines messages for corporate-action notification, instructions, status and movement confirmation. For example, `seev.031` is a Corporate Action Notification, `seev.033` is an instruction and `seev.036` is a movement confirmation. [ISO 20022 message definitions](https://www.iso20022.org/iso-20022-message-definitions?business-domain%5B0%5D=6&search=seev)

The document could look like this:

```text
CORPORATE ACTION NOTIFICATION

Notice Reference: CA-2026-0821-VR
Event Type: Rights Issue
Event Classification: Voluntary

Issuer: Verdant Renewables SA
Security: Verdant Renewables Ordinary Shares
ISIN: FR001400VRN5

Announcement Date: 20 August 2026
Ex-Date: 23 August 2026
Record Date: 24 August 2026

Entitlement:
One new share for every five existing shares

Subscription Price:
EUR 8.50 per new share

Available Options:
Option 1 – Exercise the rights
Option 2 – Sell the rights
Option 3 – Allow the rights to lapse

Default Option:
Rights will lapse if no instruction is received

Market Deadline:
29 August 2026 at 10:00 CEST

Payment Date:
5 September 2026

Important Information:
Instructions received after the market deadline may be rejected.
Fractional entitlements will be rounded down.
```

It may not always be this neat. The ratio might be on page one, the deadline on page two and restrictions inside a long paragraph.

That messiness is one reason we are building the Copilot.

## 2. Where does each piece of information come from?

This is the most important thing to understand: the Copilot needs several different data sources.

The corporate-action notice does not contain everything.

| Information needed | Real-life source | POC source |
|---|---|---|
| What event happened | Custodian, issuer or market notice | Synthetic notice PDF |
| Event dates, rate and options | Corporate-action notice | Synthetic notice PDF |
| Security details | Security master or reference-data system | `securities.csv` |
| Which funds hold the security | Accounting, custody or investment-book system | `holdings.csv` |
| How operations should process it | Internal SOP and control documents | Synthetic SOP PDF |
| What election was selected | Portfolio manager or authorised decision-maker | Entered in the POC |
| Approval | Operations reviewer/checker | Demo user action |
| What was instructed | Custodian instruction system | Simulated instruction |
| What actually settled | Custodian statement or settlement confirmation | `actual-settlement.csv` |
| What happened during processing | Workflow and audit system | POC database |
| What the correct answer should be | POC test design | Golden-answer files |

DTCC’s corporate-action materials similarly distinguish announcement data, entitlements and allocations, and election or instruction data. These are related but separate parts of the lifecycle. [DTCC corporate-action data dictionaries](https://www.dtcc.com/asset-services/corporate-actions-processing/scenarios)

## 3. What comes from the notice?

The notice tells us about the event itself.

The Copilot should read the notice and extract:

- Event reference
- Event type
- Mandatory or voluntary
- Issuer
- Security name
- ISIN
- Announcement date
- Ex-date
- Record date
- Payment date
- Market deadline
- Rate, ratio or price
- Currency
- Available options
- Default option
- Fractional-entitlement rule
- Restrictions

The notice does not tell us that the “European Opportunities Fund” owns 100,000 shares. That information comes from the organisation’s internal holdings data.

## 4. What are the important dates?

### Record date

The date used to determine which holders are entitled to participate.

### Ex-date

The date from which buying the security generally no longer carries the upcoming entitlement.

For dividends, the record date and ex-date help determine who should receive the payment. [Investor.gov explanation of record and ex-dividend dates](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/ex-dividend-dates-when-are-you-entitled-stock-and)

### Market deadline

The final deadline given by the custodian or market for submitting an election.

### Internal deadline

The organisation normally sets an earlier deadline so there is time to review, approve and submit the instruction.

For example:

```text
Market deadline: 29 August at 10:00 CEST
Internal deadline: 28 August at 10:00 CEST
```

### Payment or settlement date

The date on which cash or securities are expected to arrive.

## 5. What comes from the holdings file?

The holdings file answers:

> Who actually owns the affected security, and how many units are eligible?

Example:

```csv
fund,account,isin,position_date,eligible_quantity
European Opportunities Fund,CUST-6632,FR001400VRN5,2026-08-24,100000
Sustainable Growth Fund,CUST-7741,FR001400VRN5,2026-08-24,50000
Global Technology Fund,CUST-4410,US24703D1072,2026-08-24,80000
```

The Copilot matches the notice and holdings using the ISIN:

```text
Notice ISIN: FR001400VRN5
                    ↓
Find every eligible holding with FR001400VRN5
                    ↓
European Opportunities Fund: 100,000
Sustainable Growth Fund: 50,000
```

The holdings must be dated correctly. A position recorded after the eligibility date should not automatically be used.

## 6. What does the Copilot calculate?

Suppose the notice says:

```text
One new share for every five existing shares
Subscription price: EUR 8.50
```

The system finds:

```text
European Opportunities Fund: 100,000 shares
Sustainable Growth Fund: 50,000 shares
```

It then calculates:

### European Opportunities Fund

```text
Entitlement = 100,000 ÷ 5
            = 20,000 new shares

Full subscription cost = 20,000 × EUR 8.50
                       = EUR 170,000
```

### Sustainable Growth Fund

```text
Entitlement = 50,000 ÷ 5
            = 10,000 new shares

Full subscription cost = 10,000 × EUR 8.50
                       = EUR 85,000
```

These calculations should be performed by normal application code. The AI extracts the ratio and price; it should not mentally calculate and return an unsupported answer.

## 7. What comes from the SOP?

The notice tells us what the event is.

The SOP tells us how our organisation must process it.

For example, our synthetic SOP might say:

```text
1. All extracted terms must be reviewed by an operations analyst.

2. Internal deadlines must be at least 24 hours before the
   custodian market deadline.

3. Voluntary elections require maker-checker approval.

4. The person preparing an election cannot approve the same election.

5. Instructions must not be prepared until holdings and
   calculations have been validated.

6. Settlement differences must create an exception task.

7. Manual corrections must include a reason.
```

The Copilot uses these rules to generate tasks and block unsafe actions.

For example:

```text
Event is voluntary
        ↓
SOP says voluntary events need maker-checker approval
        ↓
Create “Obtain checker approval” task
        ↓
Block instruction until approval is complete
```

## 8. What is an election?

An election is the holder’s choice for a voluntary corporate action.

For the rights issue, the available choices might be:

- Exercise all rights
- Exercise some rights
- Sell the rights
- Allow the rights to lapse

The Copilot should explain the options and calculate their operational impact.

It must not decide which option is best for the fund.

The decision is entered by an authorised person. Operations then prepares and checks the instruction.

A normal mandatory cash dividend does not require an election. Cash is paid according to the announced terms.

## 9. What does settlement data represent?

After the event is processed, cash or securities should arrive.

The expected settlement comes from our calculation.

The actual settlement comes from the custodian.

Example:

```text
Expected dividend cash: GBP 191,250
Actual custodian cash:  GBP 189,250
Difference:              GBP 2,000
```

The Copilot should say:

```text
Status: Reconciliation break
Type: Under-settlement
Difference: GBP 2,000

Suggested checks:
- Verify eligible quantity
- Check whether withholding tax was applied
- Confirm the announced dividend rate
- Check whether one account settled separately
- Contact custodian if the difference remains unexplained
```

The AI may suggest investigation steps, but it should not invent the reason for the difference.

## 10. How do we prepare the demo data?

We should build the demo data backwards from the correct answer.

### Step 1: Decide the business truth

Before creating a PDF, write down the correct event facts.

For the rights issue:

```text
Event ID: EVT-RIGHTS-001
Issuer: Verdant Renewables SA
ISIN: FR001400VRN5
Event: Rights issue
Classification: Voluntary
Record date: 24 August 2026
Ratio: 1 new share for every 5 existing shares
Subscription price: EUR 8.50
Market deadline: 29 August 2026 at 10:00 CEST
Default option: Lapse
Fraction rule: Round down
```

This is our master truth.

### Step 2: Create the synthetic notice

Turn those facts into a realistic two- or three-page PDF.

Spread the facts across the document:

- Page one: issuer, ISIN, event and dates
- Page two: ratio, price and options
- Page three: deadline, default option and restrictions

Now the Copilot has a realistic document to analyse.

### Step 3: Create matching security data

```csv
security_id,isin,security_name,currency,market
SEC-001,FR001400VRN5,Verdant Renewables SA,EUR,France
```

This confirms that the security exists internally.

### Step 4: Create matching holdings

```csv
fund,account,isin,position_date,eligible_quantity
European Opportunities Fund,CUST-6632,FR001400VRN5,2026-08-24,100000
Sustainable Growth Fund,CUST-7741,FR001400VRN5,2026-08-24,50000
```

Also add a few holdings that should not match:

- Different ISIN
- Closed account
- Position after the eligibility date
- Zero quantity

This tests that the system does not include incorrect accounts.

### Step 5: Manually calculate the correct answer

Create the answer key:

```csv
account,eligible_quantity,entitlement,full_subscription_cost
CUST-6632,100000,20000,170000
CUST-7741,50000,10000,85000
```

These are not values for the UI to blindly display. They are used to test whether the application calculates correctly.

### Step 6: Create the SOP

Write a small synthetic internal procedure covering:

- Validation
- Deadline rules
- Maker-checker approval
- Instruction preparation
- Reconciliation
- Escalation

### Step 7: Create election examples

For example:

```text
CUST-6632: Exercise all 20,000 rights
CUST-7741: Exercise 5,000 and allow 5,000 to lapse
```

Expected funding:

```text
CUST-6632: 20,000 × EUR 8.50 = EUR 170,000
CUST-7741: 5,000 × EUR 8.50 = EUR 42,500
```

### Step 8: Create settlement results

Create one correct result:

```text
Expected: 20,000 shares
Actual: 20,000 shares
Result: Matched
```

Create one broken result:

```text
Expected: 5,000 shares
Actual: 4,800 shares
Difference: 200 shares
Result: Under-settled
```

### Step 9: Create users

Use synthetic people:

- Aisha Mehta — Operations Analyst
- Daniel Reed — Reviewer
- Maya Shah — Operations Manager

Aisha prepares the election. Daniel approves it. Maya monitors the dashboard and escalations.

### Step 10: Load everything into the POC

The POC database contains:

- Securities
- Funds
- Accounts
- Holdings
- Preloaded event cases
- Tasks
- Users
- Settlement records
- Audit entries

But the hero rights-issue case should start from the beginning, so we can upload the notice and demonstrate the Copilot doing the work.

## 11. What exactly happens during the demo?

The presenter follows this flow:

```text
1. Upload rights-issue PDF
2. Copilot identifies it as a voluntary rights issue
3. Copilot extracts dates, ratio, price and options
4. Analyst checks the notice evidence
5. Analyst corrects or validates the extracted fields
6. System matches the ISIN with eligible holdings
7. System calculates entitlements and funding
8. Analyst enters elections
9. Reviewer approves them
10. System generates a simulated instruction
11. Settlement data is loaded
12. System finds one matched result and one break
13. Audit trail shows the complete history
```

## 12. The easiest way to remember the data

Each source has one clear purpose:

```text
Notice       = What the company announced
Security     = What instrument it relates to
Holdings     = Who is affected
SOP          = How our organisation must process it
Election     = What choice was made
Calculation  = What we expect
Settlement   = What actually happened
Audit        = Who did what and when
Golden answer = How we prove the POC is correct
```

We should never mix these together.

The notice should not contain our internal holdings. The holdings file should not contain corporate-action terms. The SOP should not decide the election. The AI should not invent any missing financial information.

That separation is what makes the POC logical, explainable and believable.
